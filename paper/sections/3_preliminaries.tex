\section{Problem Setup}
For a matrix $W_t\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ with stochastic gradient $G_t$, GrassRoute stores $U_t\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$ with $U_t^\top U_t=I_r$. The routing object is $[U_t]\in\mathrm{Gr}(r,d)$, represented by $P_t=U_tU_t^\top$. Gradient routing is the orthogonal decomposition
\begin{align}
Z_t&=G_tU_t,\qquad G_t^\parallel=Z_tU_t^\top,\\
G_t^\perp&=G_t-G_t^\parallel.
\end{align}
The two routed moments store $2mr$ entries; the basis and subspace momentum store $2dr$. Parameters outside routing require additional fallback states. We report this persistent optimizer state separately from peak GPU memory.

With $\mathcal F_t$ denoting the pre-batch state, the normalized implementation tracks the leading eigenspace of
\begin{align}
C_t&=\mathbb E_t\left[\frac{G_t^\top G_t}{\|G_t\|_F^2+\varepsilon_c}\right],\\
\Phi_t(U)&=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(U^\top C_tU).
\end{align}
This eigenspace generally differs from that of $S_t=\mathbb E_t[G_t^\top G_t]$. Let $\Delta_t$ denote the rank-$r$ eigengap of the covariance under analysis. Principal angles to its leading subspace define $R_t=(\sum_i\theta_i^2)^{1/2}$ and $\theta_{\max,t}=\max_i\theta_i$. The local theory assumes a positive eigengap and angles bounded away from $\pi/2$ over the analyzed interval. Small changes between consecutive bases do not establish either condition. Captured sample-gradient energy and gaps measured in compressed coordinates likewise do not determine the population eigengap.
