\section{Conclusion}
GrassRoute stores adaptive statistics in a moving low-rank subspace while
retaining an update in every gradient direction. The analysis connects local
Grassmann geometry to tracking error, transported Adam dynamics, and
conditional generalization bounds. It separates sampling noise, target drift,
moment alignment, and adaptive bias, and identifies where the bounds are sharp
and where additional assumptions are needed.

The experiments show useful but task-dependent memory trade-offs. Archived
Burgers runs achieve lower training loss with less state; SMS classification
uses substantially less state with a small macro-F1 cost. The initial Cora
accuracy gain is not consistent across additional seeds, and the six public
tasks yield no strict primary-metric win over both AdamW and periodic SVD.
Controlled tracking experiments expose the trade-off between temporal
averaging, lag, and state cost. The appendix records all attempted comparisons,
including unfinished runs and changes in evaluation scope.
